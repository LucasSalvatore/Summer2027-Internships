## The core thesis — unchanged, and now tested in both directions

> [!important] Filter on the graduation band, not on a class year
> Expected graduation **Dec 2028 / Spring 2029** = **Class of 2029**. Programs gating on a graduation band (`Dec 2028 – June 2029`, `Class of 2029`) are yours. You hold ~3.52 GPA, an active **NJ real estate salesperson licence**, an active **life & health producer licence**, **first-generation** status, and Honors EDGE.

> [!danger] The trap cuts BOTH ways
> The known half: you are a **credit junior**, so sophomore standing-gated programs are closed. The other half: programs keyed to an *imminent* graduation are closed too. Blackstone's summer internship wants *"students in their junior year of college"* with a near-term graduation — with Spring 2029 you screen out as **two years early**, despite being a credit junior.
>
> **Any sentence naming a class year as a requirement is a hard stop in either direction.** That is why Evercore works (*"a graduation date between December 2028 and June 2029"*, no standing clause) and Morgan Stanley Early Insights does not (*"second-year undergraduate students graduating between…"* — conjunctive).
>
> 🟢 **Nov 1 — the Perella Weinberg scare resolved in his favour, and the lesson is about sources.** For two scans this file warned that PWP's New York requisition might pair his band with *"penultimate-year undergraduates (rising seniors)"* — the Morgan Stanley shape. **The posting has now been read on PWP's own system and contains no standing clause at all:** *"Completing an undergraduate degree between December 2028 - June 2029."* He is eligible; deadline Jan 5, 2027.
>
> The "penultimate-year" phrasing came from a third-party guide, which applies the same gloss to **Evercore**, whose posting also contains no standing clause. So aggregator standing-glosses are **wrong in the restrictive direction** — and trusting one would have cost two New York boutique seats. Read the employer's own sentence. Only that.
