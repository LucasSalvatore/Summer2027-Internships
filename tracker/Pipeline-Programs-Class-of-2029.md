> [!danger] The trap cuts both ways — and the rule needs a third clause
> **Known half:** he is a credit junior, so programs gating on **sophomore standing as of application** are closed. **Second half:** programs keyed to an *imminent* graduation are closed too — Blackstone wants *"students in their junior year of college"* with a near-term graduation, and with Spring 2029 he screens out as **two years early**.
>
> 🆕 **Third half, learned Nov 8 — ask WHEN the standing clause is measured.** A clause measured at **internship time, relative to graduation**, describes him exactly and is not a gate:
> - **Jefferies:** *"graduating from December 2028 to June 2029"* **and** *"be completing the junior year."* For a Spring 2029 graduate, AY 2027–28 *is* the junior year. The clauses agree.
> - **LionTree:** *"for rising seniors."* Rising senior in Summer 2028 = a Spring 2029 graduate.
> - **Greenhill/Mizuho:** *"in the penultimate year"* with *"a graduation date between December 2028 and June 2029."*
> - **UBS Tomorrow's Talent:** *"second-to-last year of undergraduate study"* — **this file already accepted this exact construction in his favour.**
>
> The genuinely disqualifying shape is measured **now**, against a fixed class year: Morgan Stanley Early Insights (*"second-year undergraduate students graduating between…"*), MLT (*"College sophomore graduating in…"*), Lincoln International (*"(undergraduate students in their sophomore year of study)"*). **Read the standing clause and the band together.** Mechanical rejection on the words "junior year" or "rising senior" has now cost more seats than it has saved.
