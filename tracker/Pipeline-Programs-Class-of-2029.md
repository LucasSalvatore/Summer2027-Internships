## The core thesis — now with the half that was missing

> [!important] Filter on the graduation band, not on a class year
> Expected graduation **Dec 2028 / Spring 2029** = **Class of 2029**. Programs gating on a graduation band (`Dec 2028 – June 2029`, `Class of 2029`) are yours. You hold ~3.52 GPA, an active **NJ real estate salesperson licence**, an active **life & health producer licence**, **first-generation** status, and Honors EDGE.

> [!danger] The trap cuts BOTH ways
> The known half: you are a **credit junior**, so sophomore standing-gated programs are closed. The other half: programs keyed to an *imminent* graduation are closed too. Blackstone's summer internship wants *"students in their junior year of college"* with a near-term graduation — with Spring 2029 you screen out as **two years early**, despite being a credit junior.
>
> So the filter is neither "sophomore" nor "junior." **Any sentence naming a class year as a requirement is a hard stop in either direction.** Band wording is the lane. That is precisely why Evercore works (*"a graduation date between December 2028 and June 2029"*, no standing clause) and Morgan Stanley Early Insights does not (*"second-year undergraduate students graduating between…"* — conjunctive).
>
> **Oct 25 addition — the conjunctive trap is not hypothetical, and it is now live again.** Perella Weinberg's 2028 US Advisory requisition (New York among its offices) appears to be up, and the one available reading of PWP's postings pairs his band with *"penultimate-year undergraduates (rising seniors)"*. If that pairing is in the posting, it is Morgan Stanley all over again. **Read the sentence; do not apply off a summary.** See the watch list in [[Internship-Application-Tracker]].

> [!success] NEW — an ARGUS grant exists, and it is large
> The **ICSC Foundation ARGUS Certification Course Grant** states it *"can lower the cost of this course by up to $375"* — taking a $450 course to as little as **$75**. Eligibility: *"You must be an ICSC student member in order to receive this grant."* Applicants also certify whether they received need-based aid in the past academic year.
>
> ⚠️ **The only grant document reachable is from the 2022 cycle** (session *"January 9-10"*, application *"due December 1, 2022"*). The grant's existence and the $375 figure are solid; **those dates are not 2027 dates.** What they establish is a *pattern*: winter sessions have historically run in early January with the grant due about five weeks prior. If 2027 follows it, the grant deadline lands in **early December 2026** — which is why the ICSC membership needs to be in hand now, not in January.

> [!danger] The leasing job can disqualify you from both memberships — and now from the $50 conference rate too
> ICSC: *"An individual who is employed full-time is not eligible for a student membership."* CREFC: must be a full-time student and *"not employed full time."* Your income plan is a **full-time leasing-agent role**.
>
> The chain is: **full-time job → no ICSC student membership → no $50 ICSC New York rate (the alternative is $2,175+), no ARGUS student course, no ARGUS grant, and no CREFC membership.** Memberships run to graduation once held, so the fix is sequencing, not sacrifice: **lock both memberships in before any offer start date.** This is the single most expensive unforced error available in the whole plan.

> [!tip] ARGUS sequence — corrected Oct 25, the prerequisites are AND, not OR
> Earlier scans flagged that ICSC's two pages appeared to contradict each other on the prerequisite. **Resolved: both conditions apply.**
>
> 1. *"Participants must be ICSC Student Members at the time of registration."*
> 2. *"All participants must demonstrate previous experience with financial modeling by submitting one of three documents in their application."*
>
> So the free WSO Real Estate Modeling certificate satisfies **condition 2 only** — it does not substitute for membership. Order: **WSO cert ($0) → ICSC Student Membership ($50) → ARGUS grant application (~early Dec, if the pattern holds) → ARGUS registration ($450, or ~$75 with the grant).**
