## The standing problem, stated honestly in both directions

> [!danger] For consulting specifically, the credit-junior standing costs more than it did in finance
> In finance, the graduation band did most of the gating, so the standing gate cost him a handful of insight days. **Consulting is different.** Almost the entire early-access apparatus in consulting is built on *class standing*, not graduation year — freshman and sophomore leadership summits, diversity accelerators, first-year immersions. That apparatus is how first-generation candidates without a target-school network get into MBB pipelines early. He is locked out of nearly all of it, and the first-gen criterion he *does* meet (Bain First Forward) is bolted to a sophomore clause he does not.
>
> That is a real cost. It should not be talked around.

> [!success] Three of the programs he was locked out of have been canceled for everyone
> **BCG Growing Future Leaders — canceled. McKinsey Sophomore Summer Business Analyst — canceled. Deloitte Discovery Intern (Consulting) — canceled. BCG Bridge to Consulting — canceled.** All four are carried in [[Pipeline-Programs-Class-of-2029]] as "track, never apply" sophomore gates. They are no longer gates; they are closed doors for the whole class. That doesn't give him anything, but it does mean the gap between him and a same-age sophomore is narrower than the old file implied.

> [!success] And the main cycle does not care about standing at all
> The decisive application — the junior-summer consulting internship, **Summer 2028** — is gated on graduation year. So are the three best pipeline programs below. He is eligible for all of them. The structural disadvantage is in the *early-access* layer, not in the layer that actually produces offers.

## The tech second discipline — his call, and what it needs to work

> [!note] Decided Nov 3: no double major, no minor
> His words: *"im not doubling major i think i can break into both fields by learning a few computer model languages mainly python, prompt engineering, and SQL."* The Finance-only decision in the project instructions **stands**.

> [!danger] The consequence has to be stated plainly
> Without credits backing it, **nothing on a transcript proves the tech skill**. A self-taught claim with no artifacts is invisible to a recruiter and worse than silent in an interview, because it invites a question he can't answer with evidence. Choosing self-study means choosing to substitute **shipped, public artifacts** for credits. That's a real trade and it works, but only if the artifacts actually exist.
>
> Target: **3–4 projects with public repositories** by the time Summer 2028 applications go out.

### The stack, ranked

| Priority | Skill | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **SQL** | The highest-leverage item on his list and he had it right. Consulting work is data pulls and client databases; SQL appears in Deloitte/Accenture analytics interviews directly. It is also the fastest to reach competence in. |
| **2** | **Python** | Correct, and the right second. Weight it toward **pandas** and data work, not software engineering — that is what consulting actually uses. |

> [!warning] Correction on "prompt engineering"
> It is not a credential and no longer a differentiator — it is assumed competence, like using a search engine. **Nobody is hired for prompt engineering in 2026.** What *is* a differentiator is having **built something** with an LLM API: a tool that takes input, calls a model, and produces a usable output. That's a portfolio project. Reframe the line item from "prompt engineering" to "built and shipped an LLM-backed tool."

> [!danger] Cut quantum computing
> He listed it. It should come off the plan. Quantum computing is a physics and PhD-level research field; the undergraduate on-ramp is graduate quantum mechanics and linear algebra, not a course. The "quantum consultant" role is a handful of PhD seats at IBM and a few labs — it is not a consulting lane that recruits undergraduates, and it will not appear on any Class of 2029 job posting. Months spent there buy nothing for consulting recruiting and come directly out of SQL, Python and case prep, which buy a great deal. **If the interest is genuine, keep it as a hobby. It does not belong in the recruiting plan.**

### Project ideas that use the edge he already has

Generic portfolio projects (a to-do app, a weather dashboard) signal nothing. His differentiator is that **he holds an active NJ real estate salesperson licence** — almost no consulting applicant does. Build at that intersection:

1. **Rent comparables analyzer** — pull listing data for a NJ submarket, normalize it, output a comp set with price-per-square-foot distribution. Python + pandas + SQL, and it is a real estate practitioner's tool.
2. **Lease-vs-buy calculator** — inputs, amortization, breakeven. Demonstrates financial modeling *and* code.
3. **Leasing-commission model** — ties directly to the leasing-agent job and gives him something to show that employer too.
4. **One LLM-backed tool** — e.g. a document summarizer for lease agreements. Replaces the "prompt engineering" line with evidence.

Each one is a GitHub repo, a README, and a two-minute explanation. That set is a portfolio, a set of consulting case anecdotes, and a reason a recruiter remembers him.

## Case interview preparation

> [!tip] The engine is free; the paid tools are optional
> Case prep is a partner sport. The single highest-value resource is **another person to case with**, and that costs nothing — through RTC, 180DC, or a peer-matching platform's free tier. Buy a tool only after the free ones run out, which for most people is months in.

| Platform | 2026 price | Free tier | Verdict for him |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crafting Cases** | Courses from $70 | **Free 7-day Case Interview Fundamentals course** | 🟢 **Start here.** Teaches building custom structures rather than memorizing frameworks, which is what interviewers actually reward. |
| **PrepLounge** | Premium €79 one-time for 1 year | **Basic tier with 10 free peer meetings** | 🟢 **And here.** The 10 free peer sessions are the single most valuable free thing in case prep. |
| **Management Consulted** | All Access $700/yr; coaching $350/hr | **Free Case Foundations course** with a free account | 🟡 Take the free course. The paid tiers are not justified at this stage. |
| **RocketBlocks** | $35/mo or $155/yr | 7-day trial | 🟡 Worth it **later**, and only for a specific weak spot — case math or chart reading. |
| **CaseCoach** | $49/mo or $199/12mo (one source gives €43/€171 — verify) | 10-day trial incl. 10+ classes, 8 cases, 50+ drills, up to 5 peer mocks | 🟡 The best structured video course if he wants one. Trial first. |
| **Road to Offer** | $49/mo, $120/90 days, $249/yr | 1 graded case, daily drills, a resume review, **one free run of four Solve formats** | 🟡 Note: the publisher of this comparison is also one of the platforms in it. The free Solve runs pair well with McKinsey's own early Solve access. |

**Sequence:** free Crafting Cases course → PrepLounge free peer sessions → RTC/180DC case partners → buy one paid tool only if a specific weakness persists. Start the free courses in **December 2026**, not January, so the March 2027 deadline isn't the first time he sees a case.

## Wall Street Oasis — the verdict

**What it is:** a finance-career platform founded in 2006, combining a free community with paid courses and a paid recruiting accelerator called WSO Academy.

### Free tier — yes, and he is already using it

Free: forums, salary and bonus data, company reviews, interview question pages, and **five free crash courses — Excel, DCF, real estate, VC and PowerPoint.**

This is already load-bearing. The **WSO Real Estate Modeling crash course** is what satisfies ICSC's second ARGUS prerequisite (*"demonstrate previous experience with financial modeling"* via *"a financial modeling course certificate from an accredited institution"*). The Excel course supports priority 3 above. Both are free. **Make the account.**

### WSO Academy — no

| Option | Price |
|---|---|
| Paid up front | **$7,000** |
| 10-month plan | **$8,000** ($2,000 down, then $600/mo) |
| 24-month plan | **$10,600** ($1,000 down, then $400/mo) |

Reasons to decline, in order of weight:

1. **It is the wrong field.** WSO is, by its own reviewers' description, *"heavily IB-centric."* He has just made consulting the primary lane. A $7,000 investment-banking accelerator is not a consulting plan — and the consulting-native tools in the table above cost $0–$249.
2. **The price against his situation.** He is working a leasing-agent job specifically to build savings through a gap semester, on a housing voucher transition, while clearing scholarship deadlines. $7,000 is not a marginal expense.
3. **The claims are unaudited.** The 88% placement rate, the 4.82 rating and the alumni salary figures are self-published with no third-party verification.
4. **No recognized credential comes out of it.** The modeling courses carry no certification with the resume weight of, say, CFI's FMVA.
5. **The discount is permanent**, which means the crossed-out "list" prices are not real prices, and any urgency framing around them is manufactured.
6. **The guarantee is conditional** on completing every module and meeting outreach targets. Anyone considering it should get those terms in writing first.

**Verdict: free account yes, Academy no.** If a paid resource ever becomes right, it should be a consulting one, it should cost under $250, and it should come after the free tiers are exhausted.
